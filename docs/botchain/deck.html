02

The problem

Agents can't onboard humans.

Every system that needs to pay a person — an AI agent settling a bounty, a protocol running rewards, a community paying contributors — hits the same wall. The recipient has no wallet, and nothing can walk them through creating one.

Wall one

The claim step

Install a wallet. Write down twelve words. Buy gas. Paste a forty-character address you cannot read. Most people quit here — and 20–40% of all reward and airdrop value is never collected as a result.

Wall two

Gas economics

Social payments are small payments. On most chains a $0.50 tip costs more in fees than it delivers, so consumer payments have never worked at scale — the economics forbid it before the UX even matters.

Wall three

No addressing layer

Autonomous agents can transact with contracts, but they cannot resolve "pay this person." There is no identity-addressed payment rail between agents and the humans who do work for them.

An agent economy that cannot pay people is not an economy.

08

Who this is for

Two markets, one primitive.

Market one

Agent & protocol payouts

Task bounties, data labelling, RLHF work, referral splits, community rewards. Anywhere software must pay a human, the recipient's missing wallet is the bottleneck. One API call with a handle removes it.

Distribution is the payment itself: every payout publicly names a wallet that did not exist a second earlier.

Market two

Emerging-market consumer payments

Nigeria first. Our users do not own hardware wallets — they own a phone and a social handle, and that is the entire account model.

They are reached as recipients, not as signups: a community manager paying 20–50 contributors brings 20–50 funded wallets at zero acquisition cost, all of whom arrive already holding a balance.

The same contract serves an agent paying a worker and a person paying a friend.
